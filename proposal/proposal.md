# Project Proposal

## Problem description

### Context

- Roughly 4.5 billion people and 1/4 of the world's food crops affected by Aflatoxin contamination. 
- Disproportionately impacts smallholder farmers.
- Aflatoxin causes several billion dollars annually in crop losses, increased health care costs and trade restrictions.
- Contamination is amplified by poor drying practices, when maize retains moisture or is exposed to rain.

### Existing Product

- Current system draws energy from a solar cell to charge a car battery.
- The car battery drives, through a controller, a low voltage motor and fan akin to a desk or extractor fan.
- This inflates a plastic bag which contains the grain in an even layer of ~50mm.
- The air can then be vented through exhausts to expel moisture.
- The current solar cell provides 150W in ideal conditions at 17.5V and 3.43A.
- The battery appears to be a conventional 12V car battery.

## Technical Proposal

- Investigate methods of introducing heat to the inflowing air for quicker/more effective drying.
    - Investigate using the fan motor heat by ducting over a heat sink.
    - Investigate using heating elements similar to those of a hair drier to control how much energy is used to heat the incoming air and ensure no energy is wasted in venting warm air from the exhaust.
    - Investigate using thermal battery concepts to bypass inefficiencies in battery storage for night time heating.
    - Investigate methods of managing energy harvested in the day to retain enough for nigh time running.
    - Investigate focusing the sun's energy directly onto the incoming air (likely mirrors).
- If there is time/scope, investigate methods of ensuring even drying of the maize.
    - A raised perforated floor may help hot air flow through the maize rather than just over, helping to dry the maize throughout, but may lead to complexities with managing the airflow.
    - A tumbler could be used to periodically mix the maize, but this may run into energy and space constraints.

## Solution Evaluation in Wider Context

- The SOLARSAFE system is unlikely to be bought, owned, and operated by a single farm/farmer, but instead by an entire community or co-operative.
- The maize harvest is a seasonal event, so there is a relatively short period of time for all the members of the community to extract and treat their maize.
- Aflatoxin causing fungus primarily enters the maize through contact with soil which is commonly the first step in the traditional harvest as some of the moisture from the crop is removed as it lies on the ground in the sun before being transported to storage for further drying.
- By adding heat to the process, the drying operation becomes more effective and rapid.
- Additionally, extending operation into the night allows for longer operational time so a greater quantity of maize can be processed in a given day.
- The previous two points are vital for ensuring that the machine can be passed around the community sufficiently quickly to allow all members to use it for their harvests without wasting crops by resorting to the traditional methods that expose the maize to aflatoxin, or waiting too long after ideal harvest and letting crops spoil in the ground.
- More effective drying would also be a step towards the maize processed with this machine becoming of a sufficiently high standard to meet foreign health requirements, potentially allowing high prices be saught for the maize and so economically benefitting the community using SOLARSAFE.
- Since the first step in traditional harvesting of maize is to spread it on the ground to perform inital drying, it is likely that this drying device will need to be sufficiently portable to be taken out to the field so that the maize does not weigh so much when being transported back to storage, hence cutting out the main part of the process at risk of introducing the aflatoxin.
