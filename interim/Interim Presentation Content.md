- Issues

  - The Peltier device did not fit well with the initial couple of drafts.

  - With Kevin's help (a lot of help), created a list of alternative components for some of the problem parts and used suggested alternative vendors where possible.

### Prototype Presentation

- Function
  - It was important to feature the Peltier's cold side with the air flow passing over its heat sink in order to draw water out of the air.
  - In order to maximise the value of the power dedicated to the Peltier, our theory is that having the air turn 180 degrees to pass back over the hot side of the Peltier, then through the blower to gain more heat from the motor's waste, and a further heating element, would lead to the relative humidity of the air dropping as much as possible.
  - It was important to use a blower with a high rise in static pressure in order to accommodate the pressure loss around the 180 bend.
  - Using PVC pipes both shields us from accidental harm from the heating elements (should not be exceeding 60 degrees Celsius, the max rated temperature of the PVC) and allows for quick contruction or reassembly if features do not work as intended and need a redesign.
  - The original Peltier prototype draft had a vent for condensed water to escape, but angling the inlet to the water can simply run out should achieve the same thing for less work.
  - Once assembled, this provides the ideal setup for the control aspect of the project to be tested.

- Feasibility
  - The majority of the design is created from parts which should all be relatively available in Kenya.
  - The bilge pump may be harder to source far inland from the coast.
  - The Peltier is specialised, so may only be available in large urban centres, but is solid state so is unlikely to break.
  - Our intended experimental method will then determine how effective this setup is at decreasing the relative humidity of the air.

- Ability to Meet Targets
  - The target was to initially provide heat to the air, but re-evaluating to understand that we want the air to have the greatest drying potential, this prototype should meet that.
  - If the prototype can meaningfully increase drying potential, this should increase the rate and effectiveness of drying.
  - If the prototype is sufficiently effective, we can then experiment with limiting the power supplied during the day to then continue operation at night.

### Learnings

  - Static pressure rise is not a commonly available property of a commercial ducted fan, so it was important to compare use cases and estimate the design requirements for each.
  - Departmental processes are longer and more complicated than anticipated, so it was important to have alternatives ready and different workflows that were unaffected.

### Route to Completion

  - Complete assembling the prototype build by mounting the electrical components.
  - Perform an airflow test to ensure that the bilge pump can handle the 180 degree bend in the pipe.
  - Test the relative improvement in air humidity with different ratios of power delivery to each component.
